How to Close Out a Cash Register at the End of the Day

Closing out the register at the end of the day tells you what you actually took, and whether the cash in the drawer matches what the sales say it should be. It takes ten minutes once you have a routine. Here’s a simple one for a stall, cart, truck or small shop.

What “closing out” means

Closing out, also called cashing up, a daily close or a Z-report, has two parts. First, you count the cash in the drawer. Second, you compare that count with what your sales records say you should have. The difference tells you whether you gave the right change, whether anything was missed, and how the day went.

The one formula to remember

Expected cash = starting float + cash sales − cash refunds − any cash you took out during the day.

Card payments aren’t in the drawer, so leave them out of this number. They show up in your card processor’s report instead. Tips paid by card also stay out of the drawer, but tips paid in cash are part of the cash you count, so know which ones you’re counting.

A simple routine

  1. Finish the last sale. Make sure no orders are half-finished or waiting to be recorded.
  2. Count the cash. Do it somewhere calm, once, out of the customers’ sight. Sort by denomination and add it up. If you get a surprising number, count again.
  3. Take out the float. Subtract the cash you started with. What’s left is what you earned in cash. Put the float aside for tomorrow.
  4. Compare with the sales report. Your POS app or notebook gives the cash sales total. Subtract refunds paid out in cash.
  5. Write down the difference. The count is “over” if you have more than expected and “short” if you have less.
  6. Check the card total. Compare the card sales in your sales report with your card processor’s report for the same day.
  7. Bank or secure the cash. Keep a note of what you deposit.

What to do when the drawer is over or short

A small difference of a coin or two is normal, since making change by hand isn’t perfect. A bigger gap, or the same gap day after day, is worth looking into. Common causes:

  • Wrong change, especially during a rush.
  • A sale taken in cash but rung up as a card payment, or the other way round.
  • A refund paid from the drawer but not recorded.
  • Cash taken out to buy supplies, with no note.
  • A float that wasn’t counted at the start of the day.

Look at the day’s orders for any that look odd, and ask whoever worked the register. Don’t just adjust the numbers to match. Record what happened, even if it’s “short by $4, cause unknown,” so a pattern becomes visible over time.

What else to look at while you’re there

The end-of-day numbers are useful beyond the cash. Look at net sales, tips, discounts and refunds for the day, your busiest hour and your best-selling items. A week of these tells you when to open, how much to prep, and what to stock more of.

Keep a record

Keep each day’s report, either printed or saved, along with the counted cash and the deposit slip. It makes tax time and any dispute much easier.

Doing it with ioPOS

ioPOS has a Daily close screen that adds up the day for you. It’s for owners and managers, so staff using a shared iPhone with PINs won’t see sales totals.

  1. Go to the Orders tab. After at least one sale, the Close the day button (a moon icon) appears in the toolbar. Tap it.
  2. Read the top of the Daily close screen: Net collected, the number of orders and items, then the Sales section with Gross sales, Discounts, Net sales, Tax, Tips and Refunds. Payments splits the day by payment type.
  3. In the Cash section, find Expected in drawer. This is cash sales minus cash refunds for the day. It does not include your starting float, so keep the float in mind when you count.
  4. Tap Count cash and enter the total you counted. If your count includes the float, take the float off first so you’re comparing like with like.
  5. ioPOS shows Balanced, Over or Short, with the amount of the difference.
  6. Scroll for the By hour chart and Top items. Tap Share report to send or save the day’s summary.
