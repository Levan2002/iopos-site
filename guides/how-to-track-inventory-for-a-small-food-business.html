How to Track Inventory for a Food Truck or Small Cafe

Running out of an item mid-service costs you sales and annoys regulars, while over-buying costs you money in waste. You don’t need a warehouse system to avoid both. A simple stock count and a few thresholds go a long way. Here’s a practical way to do it for a food truck, a cafe or a market stall.

Decide what you track

Start small. You can track the things you sell, like croissants, bottled drinks or pies, or the ingredients behind them, like milk, flour or cups. Pick whichever gives you the most useful warning. For most small businesses:

  • Track finished items that sell out, such as baked goods, limited specials and bottled drinks.
  • Track key ingredients or supplies that stop the whole menu when they run out, such as milk, cups or buns, in your own notes or a spreadsheet if your POS tracks only sale items.
  • Leave out items that are cheap and plentiful. Tracking everything turns the count into a chore, and then it gets skipped.

Count at the same times each day

A count is only useful if it’s regular. Count your tracked items at open and at close, or at least at close. Counting at close gives you tomorrow’s starting number and shows you what sold. When you receive a delivery, add it to the count right away, with a note of what came in.

Set a low-stock level for each item

A low-stock level (sometimes called a par or reorder point) is the number at which you want a warning. Set it high enough that you can reorder or prep before you run out. A rough rule: it should cover the time it takes to get more, plus a small cushion. If you can make more croissants by tomorrow morning, a level of a few dozen might be right. If the supplier takes three days, it should be higher.

Review the levels after a few weeks. If you keep running out, raise them. If you throw away stock, lower them.

Record waste and adjustments

Dropped trays, expired goods, staff meals and samples all take stock off the shelf without a sale. If you don’t record them, your count slowly drifts away from the truth. Make a habit of noting them, with a short reason, when they happen or at close.

Use a sold-out switch

When something runs out, take it off the menu so you don’t sell what you don’t have. Staff shouldn’t have to remember what’s gone. A “sold out” mark in your POS stops it from being tapped by mistake and tells customers upfront.

Look at sales to plan the next day

Your sales history is the best guide to how much to prep. Look at how many of an item you usually sell on a Tuesday versus a Saturday, and stock for that. A weather forecast or an event nearby changes it, so adjust.

Doing it with ioPOS

ioPOS can count stock down as you sell. Inventory is part of ioPOS Pro, which starts with a 1-week free trial.

  1. Open the Items tab and tap an item to edit it. For each size, turn on Track stock, enter the amount On hand, and set Alert at or below to the level where you want a warning, then save.
  2. From then on, each sale takes the item off your count. Items that are running low are flagged in the list.
  3. Switch to the Stock section of the Items tab to see everything you track. Tap an item to open its stock screen.
  4. When a delivery arrives, tap Receive stock and enter the quantity. You can also use Scan a delivery invoice to read it from a photo.
  5. For a regular count, tap Count and enter what’s on the shelf. For waste or a correction, tap Adjust with a note and say why. The item’s History keeps each change.
  6. To stop selling an item that has run out, swipe left on it in the Items tab and tap Sold out. Swipe again and tap Available when it’s back.

With the Morning Brief turned on, ioPOS also tells you each morning if a tracked item might sell out today, based on your recent sales. Changing stock counts is for owners and managers; staff can sell and look up orders.
