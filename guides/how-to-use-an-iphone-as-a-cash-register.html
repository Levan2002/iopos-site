How to Use an iPhone as a Cash Register

For a coffee cart, a market stall, a food truck or a small shop, an iPhone can do the job of a cash register. You don’t need a counter-top terminal, just a point-of-sale app, your list of items and prices, and a way to take payment. Here’s what to set up, step by step, and what to watch for.

What an iPhone register needs to do

A cash register has a few jobs: ring up items, add the tax, take payment, give change and keep a record of the day. A phone can do all of that with a POS (point of sale) app. Before you pick one, check that it covers the basics:

  • A list of your items, with sizes and options, that you can tap to build an order.
  • Sales tax that’s added automatically.
  • Cash handling that shows change due, and a way to record card payments.
  • Tips, discounts and split bills if you use them.
  • Receipts you can send to a customer.
  • A daily summary so you know what you took.
  • Selling when the signal is bad, which matters at markets and festivals.

Step 1: Get your items and prices into the app

Typing a whole menu into a phone takes longer than it should, and it’s where most people give up. Use a POS app that lets you import or scan a menu, or build the list in one sitting. Group items into categories, add sizes (small, medium, large) with their own prices, and add options like milk type or extra toppings.

Before you start selling, ring up a couple of test sales and check that the prices and tax come out as you expect.

Step 2: Set your sales tax

Sales tax rules depend on where you sell and what you sell. Some places tax prepared food and not packaged goods, and some don’t tax certain items at all. Check your local rules or ask your accountant, then enter the rate in your POS app. Look at one receipt to confirm it’s adding the amount you expect.

Step 3: Decide how you’ll take payment

Cash. The app shows the amount due, you enter what the customer hands you, and it shows the change. Keep a float (small bills and coins) in a box or apron so you can make change.

Cards. Most setups need a card reader or a card terminal, and some payment services can take contactless cards on the iPhone itself. Some POS apps run the card payment themselves, and some just let you record a payment you took on a separate terminal. Whichever it is, check how the app records it so your end-of-day totals match your card processor’s report.

Step 4: Tips, discounts and receipts

If you take tips, an app that shows a tip screen to the customer is quicker than asking out loud. Keep discounts simple, such as a percentage off an order or off an item, so staff don’t have to improvise.

Many customers don’t want paper. A text, an email or a QR code they scan with their phone camera is cheaper and faster than printing, and it keeps a record for refunds.

Step 5: Know what to do when the signal drops

Markets, festivals and parking lots often have poor service. Pick an app that saves sales on the phone first and syncs when it’s back online, so a dropped signal doesn’t stop the line. Cash always works offline; card payments depend on your reader and processor.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the test sales and finding a wrong price during the lunch rush.
  • Forgetting to charge the phone, or to bring a battery pack.
  • Mixing the cash float with the day’s takings, which makes the end-of-day count hard to read.
  • Setting up tax from memory instead of checking the local rules.
  • Not keeping a record of refunds.

Doing it with ioPOS

ioPOS is a point-of-sale app for iPhone. Here’s the path from a fresh install to the first sale.

  1. Open ioPOS and follow the first-run screens (Welcome to ioPOS). Enter your business name and your sales tax rate if you charge one. You can switch on Start with a sample coffee menu to try things out, then tap Start selling.
  2. On the Sell tab, tap Set up your menu. Choose Scan your menu (the Magic Setup feature) to photograph a paper menu and review the items it finds, or Add items yourself. You can edit items, sizes and options later in the Items tab.
  3. To ring up a sale, tap items on the Sell screen, or tap the microphone for Talk to Order and say the order out loud, for example “two large oat lattes and a croissant.” Talk to Order understands English. For a price that isn’t on the menu, use Custom amount.
  4. Tap the cart bar (Review sale) to check the order. Add a note or discount if you need to, then tap Charge.
  5. If you’ve turned on tips, the customer sees Add a tip? with choices including No tip and Other amount.
  6. Choose how they’re paying. For Cash, tap Exact or enter the cash received and ioPOS shows the Change due. For a card payment you took on your own terminal, choose Card on another terminal. To divide a bill, tap Split the bill.
  7. On the thank-you screen, tap Send receipt to send it by text or email or show a QR code, or tap New sale.

ioPOS works offline and syncs when you’re back online. Taking payments needs ioPOS Pro, which starts with a 1-week free trial. You can cancel in your iPhone’s Settings.
